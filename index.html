Our Story

A Century of
Quiet Devotion

In 1924, master horologist Kenji Mori opened a small workshop in the Higashiyama district of Kyoto. He believed that sound could carry memory — that a single melody, wound and released by hand, could outlive its maker.

A hundred years later, we carry that belief unchanged. Every music box we build begins not with metal or wood, but with a question: whose hands will hold this, and whose ears will close with its sound for the last time? That question guides every cut, every calibration, every lacquered finish.

We do not scale. We do not automate. We make 48 instruments per year — one per week — and we sign every one.

The Workshop Founded
Kenji Mori opens a twelve-tatami studio in Higashiyama, Kyoto, crafting his first music box from cypress and brass.
1962
The Silver Comb
Third-generation artisan Haruto Mori introduces the world's first sterling silver comb mechanism, doubling tonal sustain.
1998
Heirloom Certification
We establish the 100-year service guarantee — a promise that every instrument we ship can be fully restored for a century.

Centennial Collection
To mark a hundred years, we unveil twelve instruments — one for each month — each paired with an original composition.
